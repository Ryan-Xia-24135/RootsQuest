Why it matters

Why seeing the whole system matters

Skills tell you how to build. Systems thinking tells you what is worth building, and who it will affect.

The problem

School teaches the answers. It rarely teaches the system behind the question.

Most solutions only treat the surface. Students who love science are handed problems from a textbook, asked to memorize what is right, and are never shown how the pieces connect.

01

Surface-level solutions

Students are taught to fix the symptom in front of them, not to ask why the problem exists or what it is connected to.

02

Problems assigned, not chosen

Science fairs and school projects hand students a problem. Curiosity fades when the question was never theirs to begin with.

03

Nature is left out of the lab

STEM is taught as separate from the living world, even though nature has been solving hard problems for billions of years.

Beyond ecology

Regeneration is bigger than ecology

When most people hear “regenerative”, they think of forests, soil and rivers. Those matter, and nature is the model students learn from.

But the same thinking applies to the things people build: schools, cities, products, companies and the tools today’s students will invent. ROOTS Quest teaches students to ask a wider question: does this design leave the whole system, including the people in it, better off than before?

That is why regenerative design is not a niche topic. It is a way of designing that works for ecosystems and for humans together.

Beyond knowing the skills

What a student gains that a skill list can’t show

01

Seeing the whole system

Instead of fixing the symptom in front of them, students learn to ask why a problem exists and what it is connected to.

02

Asking better questions

Not just “what is the right answer?” but “what is the right question to ask?” Students choose problems that are genuinely theirs.

03

Designing for people and planet

Students practise proposing solutions that are good for ecosystems and for the communities that depend on them.
