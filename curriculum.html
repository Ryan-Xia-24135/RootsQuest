Curriculum

What students learn, build and take with them

Four weeks, four branches of engineering, and one short design prompt each week. Here is what students learn, use and take home.

  • A small pilot for 5 to 6 students
  • Four weeks, 60 minutes a week
  • No engineering or science experience needed
The 4-week plan

How the course unfolds

One branch of engineering each week, with a family showcase built into Week 4.

Week 1

Materials: things that return to the earth

Everything is made of something. Students follow a plastic bottle from made, to used, to thrown away, then redraw it as a loop where the material is reused or returns to the soil. Waste becomes food.

Inventions
mushroom-root packaging, self-healing concrete, and a fog catcher inspired by the Namib beetle.
Try it
an AI research helper on the teacher’s shared screen, used three ways: ask, check, cite. Students check two claims against a second source.
Prompt, The Material Swap
pick a plastic item and a better material. What does it replace, how is it made, and what happens at the end?
Week 2

Sensors and electronics: letting a place tell us how it is

To look after a place, we first need to know how it is doing. Students learn the sense, decide, act loop, and how a feedback loop can make things worse or help a place recover.

Inventions
river level gauges in Alberta, soil-moisture sensors, and old phones that listen for chainsaws in the forest.
Try it
a night light in a free circuit simulator: if the sensor reads dark, then the light turns on.
Prompt, The Listening Machine
one thing to measure, one place, a rule that starts “If the sensor reads…”, and what could make the sensor wrong.
Week 3

Robotics: machines with jobs, and what nature can teach

Robots suit jobs that are hard, slow or dangerous. Students ask whether a robot or a change in the system would help most, and learn to copy a strategy from nature, not just a shape.

Inventions
a solar-powered river cleaner, a robot that delivers baby corals to damaged reefs, and RoboBee, a tiny flying robot inspired by bees.
Try it
sketch and extrude one robot part in a 3D design tool, step by step with the teacher.
Prompt, The Three-Part Robot
one job and one place; a body, a sensor and a mover; one trait borrowed from a living thing; and one limit.
Week 4

Biological and medical engineering, and the showcase

Engineers also design tools that could help life heal. Students ask who a design is for, and who might be left out.

Inventions
volunteer-made 3D-printed hands, constructed wetlands that clean water with plants, and drones that deliver medical supplies.
In class, The Helper Device
pick a need and sketch an idea in eight minutes.
Showcase
families are welcome. Every student gives a two-minute pitch of their best Idea Card, with one honest trade-off, then answers a question.
Knowledge and skills

What students know and can do by the end

Knowledge is what students understand. Skills are what they can do with it. Both are tied to real inventions.

Knowledge

Engineering & regenerative thinking

  1. 01

    “Less bad” vs. “better than before”

    Explain the difference with a real example. This idea is called regenerative design.

  2. 02

    Four branches of engineering

    How materials, sensors and electronics, robotics, and biological and medical engineering solve problems, in plain words.

  3. 03

    Flows, loops and leverage points

    Draw a simple map of a system and name one place where a small change could matter most.

  4. 04

    Learning design from nature

    Borrow a strategy from a living thing, not just its shape.

  5. 05

    Designing for people

    Ask who a design is for, and who might be left out.

  6. 06

    Comparing real inventions

    Use four questions: what does it do, how does it work, who does it help, and what is the trade-off?

Skills

Technical, design & research skills

  1. 01

    Use AI responsibly

    Think first, ask a good question, check the answer against a second source, and say when AI helped.

  2. 02

    Build a simulated circuit

    Make a light sensor turn on a light in a free online circuit simulator.

  3. 03

    Sketch a part in 3D

    Draw and extrude one robot part, and give it a name and a size.

  4. 04

    Design an Idea Card

    A one-page idea with a sketch, labels, how it works, who it helps and one honest trade-off.

  5. 05

    Pitch an idea

    Share an idea in two minutes and answer one question kindly.

What students take home

Four things every student leaves with

Everything here is made by the student, and it all goes home with them.

Learning Journal

Two sentences at the end of every class: one thing they learned, and one question they still have.

Drawings and a 3D Part

A sense, decide, act drawing, a simulated sensor circuit, and a 3D sketch of one robot part.

Four Idea Cards

One a week, each with a sketch, labels, how it works, who it helps and one honest trade-off.

Two-Minute Pitch

Their best Idea Card as a one-page pitch, shared with family and friends in Week 4.
