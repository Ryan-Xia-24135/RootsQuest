# --- why / problem ---
why.problem.span1: The problem
why.problem.h21: School teaches the answers. It rarely teaches the system behind the question.
why.problem.p1: Most solutions only treat the surface. Students who love science are handed problems from a textbook, asked to memorize what is right, and are never shown how the pieces connect.
why.problem.h31: Surface-level solutions
why.problem.p2: Students are taught to fix the symptom in front of them, not to ask why the problem exists or what it is connected to.
why.problem.h32: Problems assigned, not chosen
why.problem.p3: Science fairs and school projects hand students a problem. Curiosity fades when the question was never theirs to begin with.
why.problem.h33: Nature is left out of the lab
why.problem.p4: STEM is taught as separate from the living world, even though nature has been solving hard problems for billions of years.
# --- why / beyond-ecology ---
why.beyond-ecology.span1: Beyond ecology
why.beyond-ecology.h21: Regeneration is bigger than ecology
why.beyond-ecology.p1: When most people hear “regenerative”, they think of forests, soil and rivers. Those matter, and nature is the model students learn from.
why.beyond-ecology.p2: But the same thinking applies to the things people build: schools, cities, products, companies and the tools today’s students will invent. ROOTS Quest teaches students to ask a wider question about every invention they study: **does this design leave the whole system, including the people in it, better off than before?**
why.beyond-ecology.p3: That is why regenerative design is not a niche topic. It is a way of designing that works for ecosystems and for humans together.
# --- why / beyond-skills ---
why.beyond-skills.span1: Beyond knowing the skills
why.beyond-skills.h21: What a student gains that a skill list can’t show
why.beyond-skills.h31: Seeing the whole system
why.beyond-skills.p1: Instead of fixing the symptom in front of them, students learn to ask why a problem exists and what it is connected to.
why.beyond-skills.h32: Asking better questions
why.beyond-skills.p2: Not just “what is the right answer?” but “what is the right question to ask?” Students choose problems that are genuinely theirs.
why.beyond-skills.h33: Designing for people and planet
why.beyond-skills.p3: Students practise proposing solutions that are good for ecosystems and for the communities that depend on them.

# --- curriculum / weeks ---
curriculum.weeks.span1: The 4-week plan
curriculum.weeks.h21: How the course unfolds
curriculum.weeks.p1: One branch of engineering each week, with a family showcase built into Week 4.
curriculum.weeks.span2: Week 1
curriculum.weeks.h31: Materials: things that return to the earth
curriculum.weeks.p2: Everything is made of something. Students follow a plastic bottle from made, to used, to thrown away, then redraw it as a loop where the material is reused or returns to the soil. Waste becomes food.
curriculum.weeks.dt1: Inventions
curriculum.weeks.t1: mushroom-root packaging, self-healing concrete, and a fog catcher inspired by the Namib beetle.
curriculum.weeks.dt2: Try it
curriculum.weeks.dd1: an AI research helper on the teacher’s shared screen, used three ways: ask, check, cite. Students check two claims against a second source.
curriculum.weeks.dt3: Prompt, The Material Swap
curriculum.weeks.dd2: pick a plastic item and a better material. What does it replace, how is it made, and what happens at the end?
curriculum.weeks.span3: Week 2
curriculum.weeks.h32: Sensors and electronics: letting a place tell us how it is
curriculum.weeks.p3: To look after a place, we first need to know how it is doing. Students learn the sense, decide, act loop, and how a feedback loop can make things worse or help a place recover.
curriculum.weeks.dt4: Inventions
curriculum.weeks.t2: river level gauges in Alberta, soil-moisture sensors, and old phones that listen for chainsaws in the forest.
curriculum.weeks.dt5: Try it
curriculum.weeks.dd3: a night light in a free circuit simulator: if the sensor reads dark, then the light turns on.
curriculum.weeks.dt6: Prompt, The Listening Machine
curriculum.weeks.dd4: one thing to measure, one place, a rule that starts “If the sensor reads…”, and what could make the sensor wrong.
curriculum.weeks.span4: Week 3
curriculum.weeks.h33: Robotics: machines with jobs, and what nature can teach
curriculum.weeks.p4: Robots suit jobs that are hard, slow or dangerous. Students ask whether a robot or a change in the system would help most, and learn to copy a strategy from nature, not just a shape.
curriculum.weeks.dt7: Inventions
curriculum.weeks.t3: a solar-powered river cleaner, a robot that delivers baby corals to damaged reefs, and RoboBee, a tiny flying robot inspired by bees.
curriculum.weeks.dt8: Try it
curriculum.weeks.dd5: sketch and extrude one robot part in a 3D design tool, step by step with the teacher.
curriculum.weeks.dt9: Prompt, The Three-Part Robot
curriculum.weeks.dd6: one job and one place; a body, a sensor and a mover; one trait borrowed from a living thing; and one limit.
curriculum.weeks.span5: Week 4
curriculum.weeks.h34: Biological and medical engineering, and the showcase
curriculum.weeks.p5: Engineers also design tools that could help life heal. Students ask who a design is for, and who might be left out.
curriculum.weeks.dt10: Inventions
curriculum.weeks.t4: volunteer-made 3D-printed hands, constructed wetlands that clean water with plants, and drones that deliver medical supplies.
curriculum.weeks.dt11: In class, The Helper Device
curriculum.weeks.dd7: pick a need and sketch an idea in eight minutes.
curriculum.weeks.dt12: Showcase
curriculum.weeks.dd8: families are welcome. Every student gives a two-minute pitch of their best Idea Card, with one honest trade-off, then answers a question.

# --- about / roots ---
about.roots.span1: Our name
about.roots.h21: What Does ROOTS Mean?
about.roots.alt1: A forest tree with exposed roots
about.roots.div1: Regenerative - Thinking, planning, and designing not just to ‘make things last’ or ‘a little less bad’, but to make them actively good for the ecosystem for the future.
about.roots.div2: R
about.roots.div3: Observant - Not just seeing, but slowing down, looking closely, and finding patterns and asking questions about what other people just walk by or dismiss
about.roots.div4: O
about.roots.div5: Organic - Looking to nature for technological answers and solving problems with assistance from nature’s billions of years or experience in designing solutions.
about.roots.div6: O
about.roots.div7: Thinking - Not just memorizing answers, but asking questions to carve out better questions. Not just asking what is right, but what is the right question to ask.
about.roots.div8: T
about.roots.div9: Systems - Understanding that everything is connected, seeing feedback loops, relationships, how the growth of one can stifle the growth of another.
about.roots.div10: S
