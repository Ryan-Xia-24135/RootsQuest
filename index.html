What is ROOTS Quest?

A 4-week course in engineering for an impact

ROOTS Quest is a free, youth-led course for students in Grades 6⁠–⁠8. Each week covers one branch of engineering, three real inventions and one short design prompt.

What

Engineering for an impact

Four branches of engineering, explored through real inventions and ideas of your own.

Where

Live on Zoom

Join from home with a computer, a browser, a pencil and paper. Based in the Calgary area, Alberta.

When

One branch of engineering each week, with a family showcase built into Week 4.

More about ROOTS Quest
What students learn

Four weeks, four branches of engineering

Some designs only make a problem a little less bad. Others help a place heal. Each week, students use this idea, called regenerative design, to look at one branch of engineering.

Knowledge · 01

Systems thinking

What is it? Every invention sits inside a bigger system of people, places and nature. Students draw simple maps of flows and loops, and find the one place where a small change could matter most.

  • Flows and loops: “waste is food”
  • Feedback loops that help a place recover
  • Leverage points and trade-offs
Knowledge · 02

Regenerative design

What is it? Many designs aim to be a little less bad. Regenerative design aims higher: to leave people and places better than before.

  • “Less bad” vs. “better than before”
  • Learning from nature: copy a strategy, not just a shape
  • Who is this for? Who might be left out?
Skills · 03

Real inventions, then your own ideas

How we teach it. Each week students study three real inventions with the same four questions, try a hands-on design tool, and answer one short design prompt on an Idea Card.

  • What does it do? How does it work?
  • Who does it help? What is the trade-off?
  • Using AI responsibly: ask, check, say

How the four weeks connect

  1. Week 1

    Materials

    What things are made of, and where they go afterwards.

  2. Week 2

    Sensors

    How a place can tell us how it is doing.

  3. Week 3

    Robotics

    Machines with jobs, and what engineers copy from nature.

  4. Week 4

    Bio and medical

    Tools that could help life heal, then a pitch for families.

Every student leaves with:

  • Four Idea Cards
  • Learning Journal
  • 3D sketch of a part
  • Two-minute pitch

Why it matters

Engineering for an impact means designing for people and places, not just around them.

01

Beyond knowing the skills

Tools and skills are the starting point. Students also ask what a design connects to, who it helps, and what it leaves behind.

02

Better than before

Regenerative design aims past “less bad”: materials, sensors, machines and medical tools that could help people and places heal.

03

Honest about trade-offs

Every invention has limits. Students study twelve real inventions and learn to name one honest trade-off for each design, including their own.

Who it’s for

Students who want more than a science fair

ROOTS Quest is for students in Grades 6⁠–⁠8 (ages 11 to 14) who are curious about how things are made and how they work. No engineering or science experience needed.

Science-fair students

You’ve done science fairs and loved them, and you’re ready to see how engineers turn ideas into things that help real people and places.

Curious explorers

You love exploring and following a trail to see where it leads. You notice the things other people walk past, and you want to understand why they’re there.

Builders with a specialty

You’re deep into building, like robots, rovers or circuits, and you want to see how engineers point those skills at real problems out in the world.
