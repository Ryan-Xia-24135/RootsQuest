What is ROOTS Quest?

An 8-week course that teaches students to see the whole system

ROOTS Quest is a youth-led, project-based STEM course for students in Grades 6⁠–⁠8. Students learn to understand the systems behind the problems they care about, then design responses inspired by nature.

What

A project-based STEM course

Systems thinking and regenerative design, inspired by nature.

Where

Live on Zoom

Join from anywhere with an internet connection. Based in Calgary, Alberta.

Structure

8 weeks, 1 hour a week

Each week builds toward a finished project, from first observations to a final showcase.

What students learn

Three big ideas, worked through one real project

Students don’t just hear about systems. They learn the ideas, then use them on a problem they chose themselves.

Knowledge · 01

Systems design

What is it? Every problem sits inside a bigger system of people, places, technology and nature. Systems design is learning to map those connections: what causes what, which loops keep a problem going, and where a small change matters most.

  • Mapping systems and feedback loops
  • Connecting environment, technology and society
  • Finding real points of change
Knowledge · 02

Regenerative design

What is it? Many “green” goals aim to make things last, or be a little less bad. Regenerative design aims higher: to leave a system healthier than we found it, for ecosystems and for the people who depend on them.

  • Degenerative vs. regenerative thinking
  • Learning design from nature
  • Proposing nature-inspired solutions
Skills · 03

Working through a project

How we teach it. Each student picks a problem they genuinely care about and works it from first look to final pitch: observing closely, finding their own connections, asking their own question and building a first version.

  • Observing and noticing patterns
  • Finding and evaluating sources
  • Building a lo-fi prototype and pitching it

How a student’s project unfolds

  1. Weeks 1–2

    Observe

    Slow down and look closely at the world around them.

  2. Weeks 3–5

    Connect

    Trace how people, technology and nature link to the problem.

  3. Week 6

    Question

    Choose their own question worth answering.

  4. Week 7

    Build

    Prototype a first version and get honest feedback.

  5. Week 8

    Share

    Present to a live audience of parents and peers.

Every student leaves with four finished pieces of work:

  • Process Journal
  • Systems Map
  • Lo-Fi Prototype
  • Pitch Deck

Why it matters

Regeneration isn’t just ecological. It’s design that’s good for people, too.

01

Beyond knowing the skills

Coding, building and lab skills are the starting point. The students who shape the future also ask what a design connects to, who it affects, and what it leaves behind.

02

Good for people and planet

Regenerative design isn’t only ecological. It means designing products, cities, tools and organizations that strengthen the communities and systems around them, not just cause less harm.

03

Already happening in industry

Organizations around the world, including Patagonia, Microsoft, the United Nations and the city of Singapore, are applying systems thinking and regenerative design.

Who it’s for

Students who want more than a science fair

ROOTS Quest is for students in Grades 6⁠–⁠8 (ages 11 to 14) who want to take what they already love and point it at something that matters.

Science-fair students

You’ve done science fairs and loved them, and you’re ready for a project where the question is yours and the system behind it matters as much as the result.

Curious explorers

You love exploring and following a trail to see where it leads. You notice the things other people walk past, and you want to understand why they’re there.

Builders with a specialty

You’re deep into one area of building, like aerospace, rovers or robotics, and you want to see how your skills could solve real, impactful problems out in the world.
